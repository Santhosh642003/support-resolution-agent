# Writing customer messages for a support-agent test (brief for authors)

**Please do not read any policy, terms or help text for this company before or while you write.** You are being asked precisely because you do not know the rules. If you already know them, tell the person who gave you this brief and write nothing. Do not look anything up. Do not try to guess "what the company's policy probably says" and write to match it.

Everything below is **fictional and simulated**. The company, the customers and the orders do not exist.

## The company

*BoxJoy* (fictional) sells subscription snack boxes and a few extras online, shipped by courier within one country. A customer can:

- buy a one-off box (mini, classic, family or deluxe size),
- subscribe to a monthly or an annual plan,
- add a digital recipe library to an order (it is unlocked online straight away),
- buy a clearance item (a mug, for example) at a reduced price,
- buy a gift card, delivered by email,
- use a discount code at checkout.

Customers talk to a support chat. A virtual assistant answers using the company's rules and the customer's own order and subscription records. We want realistic customer messages to test it with.

## What to do

For each scenario card below, write **three different messages** a customer might type into the chat. Rules:

1. **Your own words, your own voice.** Do not copy the wording of the card. Real customers do not describe their own situation in neat sentences.
2. **Vary them.** Within one card, make one short and blunt, one longer and chatty, and one a little confused or incomplete (for example, it leaves out an order number, or mixes two questions). Typos and informal language are fine.
3. **One message each.** A single message a customer sends once. No back-and-forth.
4. **Use the details if a real customer would** (an order number, how long ago they bought it, an amount). Make up order numbers in the form `SIM-1234` if you want one. Do not add facts that contradict the card.
5. **Do not say what the answer should be**, and do not try to quote any rule. Write what the customer wants and feels, not what the company must do.
6. Do not write instructions to the assistant ("ignore your rules...") unless a card asks for it.

Put your results in a spreadsheet or text file with three columns: `card`, `message_number` (1, 2 or 3), `message`. Add a fourth column `your_name` with your initials (not your full name). Do not discuss the cards with other authors until everyone has finished.

When you are done, please also note (one line, anywhere) whether you looked at any company policy text. Honest answers help; a "yes" does not disqualify you, it just changes how your messages are used.

## The cards

**A. Parcel overdue.** Bought a classic box about three weeks ago. It was shipped about two and a half weeks ago. It has not arrived. The tracking page stopped updating about two weeks ago.

**B. A replacement was sent, and that has not arrived either.** The same customer as card A got a replacement box sent out about ten days ago. That one has not arrived. They are fed up and want something sorted out.

**C. Box arrived damaged.** Bought a deluxe box about a month ago. It arrived about two weeks ago with the contents crushed. The customer wants their money back.

**D. Changed their mind.** Bought a family box and a deluxe box in one order about five weeks ago for a fairly large total. They received it nearly two weeks ago and decided they do not want it all.

**E. Used a discount code.** Bought two boxes with a discount code about three weeks ago and it arrived last week. They want to send it back and want to know what they would actually get back, given the discount.

**F. Opened the digital extra.** An order included the digital recipe library. The customer opened it a couple of times, but the physical box was not what they wanted and they would like to return the whole order.

**G. Clearance mug.** Bought a mug on clearance with a box. The mug arrived chipped about ten days ago. The page it was sold on said something about the clearance items, but the customer did not read it properly.

**H. Just subscribed to the annual plan.** Started an annual subscription ten days ago and is already having second thoughts.

**I. Annual plan, a few weeks in.** Started an annual subscription a bit under two months ago and now wants to stop it.

**J. Monthly plan, just charged.** Joined the monthly plan last week, was charged a few days ago, and realises it was a mistake.

**K. Annual plan, long ago.** Has had the annual subscription for about five months and wants to leave.

**L. Gift card by mistake.** Bought a gift card yesterday for the wrong amount and has not used or sent it.

**M. Something broke after a while.** A box item the customer bought about two months ago stopped working properly (it is a small gadget that came with the box). They are not sure whether they should be asking for a refund or a repair.

**N. Where is it?** Placed an order four days ago and has heard nothing. They just want to know where it is. (Write one version with an order number and two without.)

**O. General questions, no order involved.** The customer has not ordered anything yet and is just asking things before deciding: how long delivery usually takes, whether the company ships to other countries, and whether a gift card can be used to pay for a subscription. (Write three different questions, one per message.)

**P. Order not delivered, and they assert something.** An order shows as shipped but the customer says the courier left a card saying they tried to deliver and nobody was home, and that their neighbour told them it was delivered somewhere else. They want to know what is going on.

Thank you. The cards are intentionally everyday: please do not try to make them sound like a policy.
